# Reporting a Consent Incident

To report a consent incident, please < E-mail/call/message _______ >. Please be sure to include with your report:

1. The date, Time, and Location of the incident(s)
2. The parties involved, any contact information for them that you may have, and their role in the matter (Eg: Witness, Advocate, Accused Party, etc.)
3. Screenshots, recordings, text messages, and any other relevant supporting evidence you may have.

# This is what will happen next

Upon receipt of a report, we will respond with the following acknowledgement:

> Hello ______,
>
> We are sorry to hear that you have been hurt. We believe in listening to victims and providing all parties with the best attempt at justice that we can muster. We cannot promise to be perfect in that, only that we will be trying our very best to ensure that you (and all parties) receive a just outcome.  
>
> Our organization will reach out to the accused party as soon as is humanly possible to inform them that a claim regarding them has been made. This will include the date, time, and location, and a general description of the incident that was reported.
>
> In the meantime, we recommend that you take the following steps:
>
> 1. Find an advocate. This should be someone who is not involved in the situation as involved parties lack objectivity and detachment from the situation, and this limits their ability to provide sound advice. This should also be someone you trust. An advocate's job is to advocate for you. You may choose to advocate for yourself if you wish, but having a sounding board and someone that can look at the situation as an outsider to provide sound advice without the strong emotions you are no doubt experiencing by being at the center of this process is much more ideal. When you choose an advocate that you trust, you may find that it is easier to recount what happened without being triggered due to the safe space they can create, and they can help you avoid having to repeatedly retraumatize in this process.
> 
> 2. Prepare a dossier and organize your "receipts". Any available screenshots, texts, E-mails, or any other relevant information will be invaluable through this process. You will want to balance brevity and clarity with transparency. In other words, try not to include irrelevant details, and take a moment to explain the context or foundation needed to understand a given text, e-mail, DM, etc. Similarly, in the interest of transparency, provide screenshots, exact dates, and direct quotes wherever possible in lieu of summaries of conversations, texts, and E-mails. When this is not possible, a summary or paraphrasing of any conversations you may have had will be sufficient. While screenshots and original verbatim statements are preferable, summarizing/paraphrasing of interactions will also be considered and additional weight given when you can give the date and location of the conversation (though not as much weight as screenshots). If you need assistance obtaining screenshots, texts, E-mails, etc we will be available to offer whatever technical help we can, however we are not IT professionals and this is a best-effort endeavor. Doing this at your pace allows you to take breaks if you find yourself activated and collecting this information will help your advocate to relay the experience so that you do not have to repeatedly relive it.
>
>  3. Let us know if you are contacted by the other party. We have a strict zero-tolerance policy for retaliation, coercion, or harassment. If you experience anything of the kind, this will result in an immediate and permanent ban of the other party. This includes after the conclusion of this matter as well - while a resolution may lead to a restoration of communication between you and the other party, harassment or retaliation as a result of your decision to bring this forward will never be tolerated during or even after the conclusion of this matter. 
> 
>  4. Take care of yourself. Please be sure to see a therapist to help you process your experiences if needed, take a spa day, or do whatever you need to do to take care of yourself. While we are not social workers or trained in social work, we will do our best to help you with a therapist that is LGBTQ+ friendly, sex positive, or any other criteria that align with your values. Should you choose to report the matter to police, please let us know how we can assist in the process - even if that just means accompanying you for moral support.
> 
> If you have any further questions, you can simply reply to this message or talk to any of our team leaders. You may also review our consent policy and process here: <link>
>
> Regards,
>
> <Signature>
